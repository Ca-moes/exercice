Overview

A 4-day week at home: Push, Pull, Legs and Core, once each, with runs on the other days. Every session starts with the 12–15 min postural routine, then ~20–30 min of strength (Core is ~20 min).

Push, Pull and Legs each have two sessions that alternate weekly: week A does Push 1 / Pull 1 / Legs 1, week B does the 2s. The workout pages preselect the right one (odd ISO weeks are A). Alternating keeps the variety and full muscle coverage of both sessions while each pattern is trained once a week.

Once a week per muscle

Research on training frequency finds that weekly sets matter far more than how often a muscle is trained (Schoenfeld 2019; Pelland 2025). Going from twice to once a week roughly halves weekly sets per muscle, which still clears the useful minimum (~4 hard sets/week), so growth slows somewhat rather than stops. Keep sets hard (0–2 reps from failure), and if arms or shoulders lag, add a couple of curl or lateral-raise sets on Core day.

How the two sessions differ

Each pattern shares 1–2 anchor movements across both sessions (so you can track progression week to week) and differs on the rest, for variety and complete coverage. Session 1 runs in week A, session 2 in week B. The full sessions are on each workout page; in short:

Training Load Notes

  • Runs around leg day: keep Wednesday easy and put hard or long runs on Saturday/Sunday. On Thursday, lift first and run after, or leave 6+ hours between them; aerobic work only blunts leg performance for the next several hours, and lifting first gave better lower-body strength gains (Murlasits 2018). Runs after Push, Pull or Core days are fine.
  • Adding running doesn't cost muscle size or max strength in the research (Schumann 2022); the main thing to protect is recovery. Sleep and food are the limiter. If you're run down, drop a run before dropping a lifting day.
  • Keep at least one genuinely easy day a week (Sunday works: easy run, walk or rest).
  • Groin: train as normal for now, but scale or substitute the moves flagged in Leg Workout if the right inner-groin area flares. Revisit after the physio visit.
